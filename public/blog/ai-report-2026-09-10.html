Executive thesis

ASBIS Enterprises is undergoing one of the most important transformations in its history.

For years the company was primarily perceived as a distributor of smartphones, computers, processors, storage devices and consumer electronics across Central and Eastern Europe, the CIS region and emerging markets.

That description is becoming increasingly incomplete.

The global artificial-intelligence infrastructure boom has created extraordinary demand for GPU servers, processors, memory, enterprise storage and complete server systems.

ASBIS has become a direct beneficiary of this investment cycle.

Foxorox thesis: Approximately 72% of ASBIS's entire year-over-year revenue increase in Q2 2026 came from servers and server blocks alone. Server revenue reached approximately $723 million in a single quarter and became the company's largest product category. This changes the investment profile of ASBIS. The company should increasingly be viewed as an indirect European AI-infrastructure play rather than simply a traditional electronics distributor. Company directs a significant portion of its sales to post-Soviet countries, which we view as an elevated counterparty and geopolitical risk. In our assessment, adverse political, economic or regulatory developments in any of these markets could weaken the financial position of local customers or counterparties. As a result, the company may be required to recognize additional provisions or impairment charges related to doubtful receivables and the potential insolvency of selected counterparties.

6. Why AI creates demand far beyond GPUs

The artificial-intelligence investment cycle is often reduced to NVIDIA GPUs.

In reality, a GPU cluster requires an entire infrastructure stack:

  • GPU accelerators,
  • CPU processors,
  • HBM and DRAM memory,
  • enterprise SSD storage,
  • high-performance networking,
  • server motherboards,
  • power supplies,
  • complete server platforms,
  • cooling systems,
  • and data-center infrastructure.

ASBIS participates in several layers of this supply chain.

The company distributes products from major global semiconductor, hardware and infrastructure manufacturers.

This creates a fundamentally different AI investment profile from semiconductor designers.

ASBIS does not need to develop the winning AI architecture.

It can benefit from customers buying infrastructure across multiple vendors.

Instead of competing with NVIDIA, ASBIS can participate downstream in the same capital-expenditure cycle.

20. Bear case

The major risks are:

  • AI server demand proves temporary rather than structural,
  • large projects create unusually volatile quarterly results,
  • competition compresses distributor margins,
  • working-capital requirements rise sharply with revenue,
  • customers delay large infrastructure deployments,
  • geopolitical risk increases in CIS and Central Asian markets,
  • currency movements reduce PLN earnings translation,
  • and the market refuses to assign ASBIS a higher multiple because it remains a distributor.

The greatest risk is therefore not that Q2 2026 was weak.

It was exceptionally strong.

The key risk is that investors extrapolate extraordinary server growth too far into the future.
