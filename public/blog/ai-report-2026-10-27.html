1. Key findings

  1. Revenue rose from $41 million in 2023 to $4.585 billion in 2025; H1 2026 revenue reached $16.286 billion.
  2. Compute and infrastructure expense fell from 160% of revenue in 2025 to 55% in H1 2026.
  3. Q2 2026 operating income reached $974 million, an 8.43% margin.
  4. H1 2026 operating loss was $693 million and net loss $80.748 billion, much of it related to financial instrument revaluations.
  5. The company relies on third-party infrastructure while building dedicated capacity in partners’ facilities.
  6. Long-term infrastructure obligations run into hundreds of billions of dollars.
  7. Media reports discuss a $1.5–2 trillion IPO valuation; the offer price and final valuation are undisclosed.
  8. Under a 4×, 2× and then 30% annual growth scenario, 2031 revenue reaches $572.49 billion. At a $2 trillion valuation, P/S is approximately 3.5 and illustrative P/E 44–52, assuming 8.5–10% operating margins.

2. Business model

Anthropic develops Claude AI models and tools for enterprises and developers. Revenue comes from usage-based charges and subscriptions, sold directly and through cloud platforms. In H1 2026 approximately 80% of revenue was usage-based, 20% subscription-based, and 48% of sales passed through cloud marketplaces. Partners can simultaneously be suppliers, investors and competitors.

5. Why was the net loss $80.75 billion?

The H1 loss included a $72.388bn negative fair-value change in convertible notes, derivatives and SAFEs. Changes in company valuation can increase the accounting value of these liabilities without an equivalent cash payment in the period. Compare the $80.748bn net loss, $693m operating loss and $3.929bn positive operating cash flow: each measures a different aspect of performance.

8. Own or third-party data centers?

Before 2026, nearly all compute infrastructure came from three cloud providers. Anthropic is moving to a hybrid model: cloud capacity, long-term dedicated capacity leased in partners’ facilities, directly procured or leased equipment, and specialist third-party operators. Dedicated infrastructure does not necessarily mean ownership of land and buildings. Its architecture uses AWS Trainium, Google TPU, Nvidia and AMD GPUs.

Compute and infrastructure expense was $2.522bn in 2024, $7.326bn in 2025, $2.334bn in H1 2025, $8.945bn in H1 2026, and $5.558bn in Q2 2026. These amounts are not solely data-center rent.

9. Future infrastructure commitments

Reuters reported over $600bn of infrastructure and compute commitments over approximately a decade, including at least $114.5bn with Google, $110bn with Amazon and $44.9bn with Microsoft. These are future commitments, not annual bills or amounts already paid. Certain contracts include minimum commitments or take-or-pay terms. The filing also describes a five-year equipment lease with approximately $125.2bn in payments excluding interest. Do not simply add separately reported amounts: time periods and agreements may overlap.

18. Key investment risks

  1. Revenue growth falls below the scenario.
  2. Margins prove unsustainable and prices come under pressure.
  3. Compute, electricity and hardware costs rise.
  4. Long-term and take-or-pay commitments create fixed obligations.
  5. Financing, leasing, new capital requirements and share dilution reduce returns.
  6. Competition and dependence on cloud partners or concentrated customers.
  7. Accounting revaluations produce volatile net income.
  8. Capex does not capture all infrastructure payments; free cash flow may vary.
  9. Legal, data, regulatory and AI safety risks.
  10. Paying too high an entry price despite growth.

19. Investment conclusion

Anthropic has rapidly growing sales and reported positive operating income in Q2 2026. A $2tn valuation depends heavily on future growth. Under the $572.49bn 2031 revenue scenario, P/S would be 3.5 and illustrative P/E about 44–52 at 8.5–10% operating margins, before additional financing costs and other financial items. Growing into that valuation requires exceptionally strong revenue growth, durable margins, financing of infrastructure obligations and adequate cash flow after investment. Monitor operating margin, compute costs, commitments, dilution and free cash flow alongside revenue.
