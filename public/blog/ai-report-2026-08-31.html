Executive thesis

Zebra Technologies and Nordson Corporation are two very different companies, but they currently share one unusual characteristic in the Foxorox model:

Both stocks are showing bearish sentiment despite very strong recent financial results.

This creates an interesting analytical setup. The bearish view is not based on collapsing revenue, weak margins or deteriorating order books. In fact, both companies recently delivered some of the strongest quarters in their histories.

Zebra reported second-quarter 2026 revenue growth of 20.4%, adjusted EPS growth of 75.9% and a 710-basis-point expansion in adjusted EBITDA margin. Nordson reported record quarterly sales of $818 million, adjusted EPS growth of 19%, a 32% EBITDA margin and backlog growth of 35%.

Therefore the question is not:

"Are these bad companies?"

They are not.

The more useful question is:

"Have the stocks moved far enough ahead of the underlying fundamentals that near-term downside risk now exceeds near-term upside?"

That distinction is fundamental to the Foxorox bearish thesis. A strong company can still become a weak short-term investment at the wrong price.

1. What Zebra Technologies actually does

Zebra Technologies is much more than the barcode-printer company many investors still associate with the name. The company has evolved into a broad enterprise digitization and automation platform focused on what it calls intelligent operations and increasingly frontline AI.

Its technologies are used across:

  • retail,
  • warehousing and logistics,
  • manufacturing,
  • transportation,
  • healthcare,
  • field service,
  • and asset tracking.

The product portfolio includes:

  • mobile computers,
  • barcode scanners,
  • industrial and mobile printers,
  • RFID systems,
  • machine vision,
  • fixed industrial scanning,
  • workforce software,
  • asset visibility systems,
  • and automation solutions.

The strategic direction is easy to understand. More physical operations are becoming digitally visible, machine-readable and increasingly automated.

2. Zebra is becoming an AI-at-the-edge company

The term "AI" is often associated with hyperscale data centers and GPUs. Zebra participates in a different part of the architecture: AI at the physical edge.

Its systems help frontline workers and industrial environments identify:

  • what an object is,
  • where it is,
  • whether it has moved,
  • whether it has been scanned,
  • whether a package is correct,
  • whether a manufacturing process is within tolerance,
  • and what action should happen next.

The company's long-term opportunity therefore sits at the intersection of:

automation + machine vision + RFID + connected workers + physical AI.

That is strategically attractive. But the stock market has already recognized much of this improvement.

6. Memory costs are an interesting risk for Zebra

Zebra is also exposed to the same memory-cost cycle that is affecting many hardware manufacturers. Management has discussed substantial full-year memory cost pressure while working to secure supply and offset cost increases through pricing and supplier actions.

This is particularly relevant after the recent acceleration in DRAM and memory pricing.

Zebra does not manufacture memory. It buys components that ultimately become part of mobile computers, scanners and other intelligent devices.

Therefore:

higher memory pricing can become a gross-margin headwind unless Zebra successfully passes those costs to customers.

This is not currently destroying profitability, but it creates another reason to avoid assuming the unusually strong Q2 margin will rise indefinitely.

10. What Nordson Corporation actually does

Nordson is a very different business.

It is a precision technology company whose equipment is used to dispense, apply, inspect and control extremely precise amounts of materials in industrial processes.

Its end markets include:

  • semiconductors,
  • medical devices,
  • packaging,
  • consumer products,
  • industrial coatings,
  • adhesives,
  • polymer processing,
  • and advanced manufacturing.

Nordson is exactly the kind of industrial business that often remains invisible to consumers while being deeply embedded inside its customers' production lines.

11. Why Nordson is such a high-quality industrial business

Nordson's strength is not based on selling commodity machinery.

Its equipment often handles relatively small, but mission-critical parts of the manufacturing process.

For example:

  • precision adhesive dispensing,
  • fluid management,
  • electronics dispensing,
  • test and inspection,
  • medical fluid components,
  • and industrial process control.

If a Nordson system represents only a small percentage of a customer's total factory cost but failure of that system can stop production, the customer is usually less sensitive to the equipment's absolute price.

That helps explain Nordson's unusually strong margins.

19. Why a bearish signal after good earnings can make sense

Markets do not trade on whether results are objectively good or bad. They trade on the difference between:

expectations and reality.

A company can report excellent numbers and still become vulnerable if:

  • the stock already rallied heavily before or after earnings,
  • valuation expands faster than future earnings,
  • one-time benefits improve reported margins,
  • future growth is less impressive than the latest quarter,
  • or investors begin taking profits after a major positive surprise.

That is precisely the situation Foxorox is currently flagging.

26. Foxorox final conclusion

Zebra Technologies and Nordson Corporation are examples of why stock analysis cannot stop at the income statement.

Both companies recently reported excellent results.

Zebra delivered:

  • 20.4% reported revenue growth,
  • 9.2% organic growth,
  • 75.9% non-GAAP EPS growth,
  • 27.7% adjusted EBITDA margin,
  • and higher full-year guidance.

Nordson delivered:

  • record $818 million quarterly sales,
  • ~12% organic growth,
  • 19% adjusted EPS growth,
  • 32% EBITDA margin,
  • 35% backlog growth,
  • and higher guidance.

Yet both stocks currently show bearish sentiment in the Foxorox model.

Foxorox final thesis:

These are not bearish companies.
They are currently bearish stocks from a short-term risk/reward perspective.

The distinction matters.

Strong fundamentals can continue while the stock price corrects. A high-quality company can become temporarily overextended. And after strong earnings rallies, future positive surprises may already be embedded in the price.

Between the two, Nordson currently appears more demanding on valuation, while Zebra's main analytical risks are the difference between reported and organic growth, the sustainability of the extraordinary Q2 margin expansion, and rising component costs.

For long-term investors, both companies remain worth watching.

For short-term positioning, Foxorox currently indicates caution.
