1. Executive Investment Thesis

Foxorox stance: fundamentally positive, valuation-sensitive. IHG is a UK-listed international hotel company with a largely fee-based model. Hotel owners fund much of the real estate while IHG earns recurring franchise and management fees from brands, distribution and loyalty infrastructure.

Three drivers stand out: global RevPAR accelerated in the third quarter, net room growth remains near 5%, and buybacks amplify earnings-per-share growth. This is an unusually attractive combination for a travel business.

The key counterargument is valuation: the stock trades on a premium multiple that requires continued execution. Strong company fundamentals do not automatically mean the stock is undervalued.

The strongest near-term evidence is the October 22 trading update; the latest full income statement is the August 11 half-year release. Q3 was a trading update, not a full quarterly profit statement.

4. The Economics of the Asset-Light Hotel Model

IHG operates brands including InterContinental, Six Senses, Regent, Kimpton, Crowne Plaza, voco, Holiday Inn, Holiday Inn Express and Staybridge Suites. The group monetises brand reputation, reservation systems, loyalty membership and hotel-owner relationships.

The fee model has several attractions: relatively low property capital intensity, high incremental margins, diversified hotel-owner funding and room growth without IHG purchasing every building. In H1, fee business revenue rose 7% while related costs rose 4%, expanding the fee margin to 65.9%.

IHG One Rewards has more than 160 million members, strengthening repeat business and direct booking economics. This creates a platform effect, although technology investment, brand standards and customer acquisition remain real costs.

Buybacks reduce the share count and support per-share earnings, but should not be mistaken for operating growth. Leverage is meaningful: the group must maintain cash generation through weaker travel periods, and higher financing costs can erode the benefit of repurchases.

The board’s confidence in returning capital is supportive, but the sustainability of distributions depends on franchise cash flows and debt capacity.

Because forward earnings, exchange rates and share count can change, a multiple sensitivity is not a forecast. If earnings rise 13% but the forward multiple falls from 29.25x to 26x, the theoretical one-year return before dividends would be roughly flat: (1.13 × 26 / 29.25) − 1 ≈ +0.4%.

12. Why the Business Is Doing Well

  1. Pricing power: Q3 average daily rate grew 3.9%.
  2. Higher occupancy: a 0.9-point improvement reinforces rate growth.
  3. Fee operating leverage: H1 fee margin reached 65.9%.
  4. Room growth: 353,000 pipeline rooms support future fee generation.
  5. Diversification: US and EMEAA strength offsets China weakness.
  6. Shareholder returns: buybacks and dividends increase per-share value if funded sustainably.

13. Key Risks and Bear Case

  • High valuation: P/E near 38x trailing leaves little room for a demand disappointment.
  • China weakness: Greater China RevPAR fell 3.3% in Q3.
  • Travel cycle: recession, geopolitical disruption and weaker corporate travel would pressure hotel demand.
  • Debt: 2.63x net debt / adjusted EBITDA increases sensitivity to cash-flow shocks.
  • Development execution: signed hotels may open late or fail to open.
  • Buyback opportunity cost: repurchasing shares at premium multiples can destroy value if growth slows.
  • Competition: Marriott, Hilton and Accor are also growing networks and investing in loyalty.
  • Model risk: a 60.37% white-candle forecast is not a fundamental fair-value estimate.
