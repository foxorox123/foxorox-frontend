1. A Correction Creates a Question, Not an Automatic Buy Signal

Polish banks can remain profitable even when interest rates stop changing. But unchanged policy rates alone do not guarantee unchanged net interest margins, credit volumes or loan-loss provisions. Earlier rate changes can continue to affect repricing, while funding competition and customer behaviour alter the economics of the balance sheet.

2. Inflation and Expensive Fuel: How the Pressure Reaches Banks

Households have less room for new repayments

If fuel, food and other essential expenses rise faster than household income, less cash remains available for savings and loan instalments. This can reduce affordability, postpone mortgage decisions and weaken discretionary spending. Credit assessments must account for fixed expenses and the risk of lower disposable income. [18]

Deposits change hands rather than automatically disappear

A household paying a fuel retailer transfers money from one account to another. Household savings can fall without an equivalent decline in deposits across the entire banking system. For an individual bank, the key issues are customer flows, the mix of deposits and the price paid to retain funding. Payments to foreign suppliers and subsequent financing decisions can create additional effects; there is no simple one-for-one rule.

Companies may borrow differently

Higher operating costs can discourage investment borrowing while increasing short-term working-capital needs. More nominal lending is therefore not necessarily evidence of stronger investment demand. The outcome depends on whether businesses can pass costs on to customers and preserve their capacity to repay.

The most damaging combination is weaker revenue growth and higher credit losses at the same time. This article treats persistent fuel-cost pressure and slower lending as a downside scenario, rather than claiming that either has already produced a measured reduction in each bank’s 2026 profit.

3. How the Earnings Scenario Is Constructed

The starting point is reported H1 2026 consolidated net profit. The full-year assumptions below imply that H2 earns approximately 90–100% of H1 profit. This is a deliberately simple, cautious run-rate exercise: it does not forecast loan balances, interest margins, operating expenses and provisions separately.

Full-year scenario profit = reported H1 profit + assumed H2 profit
Illustrative dividend per share = scenario profit × assumed payout ratio / shares
Forward dividend yield = illustrative dividend per share / reference share price × 100%

Group profit versus distributable bank profit: The table uses consolidated earnings as a proxy. The legal basis for a dividend is the parent bank’s separate financial statements, subject to capital and supervisory constraints. Subsidiary earnings, upstream dividends and other differences can materially change the final distribution. This limitation applies to PKO BP as well as its peers.

The payout assumptions reflect recent distributions or stated intentions, rather than a uniform 75% rule. Share counts are held constant. No special dividends, release of historical capital or share repurchases are assumed.

Dividend risk can be larger than this table suggests. A bank could generate profit but retain more of it if capital needs increase or the supervisory payout allowance falls. Conversely, distributing historical reserves could lift a payment without representing stronger recurring earnings.

At a fixed dividend, a 10% lower purchase price raises the yield by approximately 11.1%. If both the share price and the expected dividend fall 10%, the yield is unchanged. A correction only improves the income proposition when the price falls faster than the sustainable cash distribution.

A full purchase decision also needs price-to-book, sustainable return on equity, cost of equity and earnings-normalisation analysis. This article does not calculate fair values or claim that the banks are cheap solely because their share prices have declined.

9. Foxorox Conclusion

The correction may justify selective buying, but the dividend arithmetic supports a watchlist rather than a blanket sector call. Pekao and PKO BP lead this cautious income screen, with illustrative gross yields of approximately 6.0% and 5.6%. Alior and Erste Bank Polska are around 4.2–4.3%, while Millennium and mBank are below 2% under the initial payout assumptions.

Stable interest rates remove one source of change; they do not remove credit, funding, tax or legal risk. Expensive fuel and pressure on household budgets could weaken borrowing capacity and repayment performance. Those channels should be tested against incoming results rather than assumed to produce an automatic collapse in deposits or bank profit.

The question is whether the entry price adequately compensates for the earnings and payout risks. A sustainable dividend at a sensible valuation can make a correction useful. A dividend estimate that falls alongside the share price offers much less protection.
